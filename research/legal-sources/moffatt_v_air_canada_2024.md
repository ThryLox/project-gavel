## 1. Facts

In November 2022, Jake Moffatt used Air Canada's website chatbot while researching bereavement fares after his grandmother's death. The chatbot advised he could book a flight at the regular fare and apply for the bereavement discount **retroactively**. Air Canada's actual policy did not permit retroactive bereavement-fare applications. Moffatt relied on the chatbot's advice, booked at full fare, and was later denied the retroactive discount by human staff.

## 2. Air Canada's Defense (rejected)

Air Canada argued the chatbot was "a separate legal entity that is responsible for its own actions," and that Air Canada could not be held liable for information the chatbot provided. The Tribunal rejected this outright.

## 3. Holding

The Tribunal found Air Canada owed a duty of care to chatbot users, breached that duty by failing to ensure the chatbot's accuracy, and was liable for **negligent misrepresentation**. The key line: Air Canada "did not take reasonable care to ensure its chatbot was accurate," and **"it should be obvious to Air Canada that it is responsible for all the information on its website,"** whether that information comes from a static page or an interactive chatbot.

## 4. Relevance to Paper A

This is the cleanest available precedent for the paper's core claim, despite being a small-claims-level tribunal decision (persuasive, not binding, outside BC) rather than an appellate contract case:
- It forecloses the "the AI is a separate actor" defense directly — courts (or tribunals) are already treating a deployed LLM agent's output as the deploying company's own statement, not a third party's.
- It was decided on **negligent misrepresentation**, not contract or agency doctrine directly — worth noting in the paper as a distinct-but-adjacent liability theory alongside the UETA §14 / Restatement §2.03 agency argument. A company can be bound *and* separately liable in tort for what its agent said.
- Low dollar amount and tribunal (not court) posture means this should be cited as **illustrative and persuasive**, not as controlling precedent — the paper should be explicit about that evidentiary weight rather than overstating it.
